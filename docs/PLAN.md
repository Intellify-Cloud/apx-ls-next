Introduction
Most leadership teams don't fail because of a shortage of talent. They struggle because of a shortage of
self-awareness.
When leaders don't understand their own patterns - how they respond under pressure, how they come
across to others, what drives their decisions - it creates friction.
Teams fragment. High performers disengage. And the organisation pays the price.
The good news is that these patterns can be changed. But first, they need to be recognised.
This guide outlines the five most common signs that a leadership team would benefit from coaching, and
practical first steps you can take.
[Note: Branded colours, fonts, and appropriate graphics/stock photo]
Page 3
Sign 1: Communication Keeps Breaking Down
What it looks like:
The same misunderstandings keep recurring. People leave meetings with different interpretations
of what was decided. Feedback is either avoided or lands badly. Leaders talk past each other
rather than to each other.
Why it happens:
Different people have very different communication styles, and most leaders have never been
taught to recognise those differences in themselves or others. The result is that well-intentioned
messages land in ways they were never meant to.
What to do:
Invest in a facilitated session that gives your team a shared framework for understanding
communication styles. Tools like the Enneagram are particularly effective because they go beyond
surface behaviour to the motivation underneath. One session can shift years of unproductive
patterns.
[Note: Branded colours, fonts, and appropriate Enneagram-related graphic/chart]
Page 4
Sign 2: Your Best People Are Showing Signs of Disengagement
What it looks like:
Strong performers are going quiet in meetings. Enthusiasm has been replaced by compliance. You
are getting the work done, but you're no longer getting the best thinking. People are doing their
jobs, but not much more.
Why it happens:
High performers disengage when they feel unseen, unchallenged, or that their growth has stalled.
Often, the leadership around them is inadvertently creating the conditions for disengagement. Not
through bad intent, but through a lack of awareness.
What to do:
Have a genuine, structured conversation with your high performers about their development. Not a
performance review - a coaching conversation. If you don't have the skills or the bandwidth to do
this well, bring in an external coach. The cost of losing a strong performer is far higher than the cost
of retaining one.

Sign 3: Conflict Is Either Everywhere or Completely Absent
What it looks like:
Either your team is in constant tension - arguments that never resolve, personalities that clash,
meetings that derail - or there is an eerie absence of disagreement. Everyone nods, nothing is
challenged, and decisions get made without genuine debate.
Why it happens:
Both are symptoms of the same underlying problem: a team that doesn't yet have the psychological
safety or the tools to handle disagreement well. Healthy conflict and robust debate, followed by
commitment, are one of the hallmarks of high-performing teams. Most teams are a long way from it.
What to do:
Work with a facilitator to establish team norms around disagreement. What does it look like when
it's healthy, and what happens when it's not? This is not a one-session fix, but a skilled facilitator
can accelerate the process significantly.

Sign 4: Leaders Are Reacting Instead of Responding
What it looks like:
Decisions are made in the heat of the moment. Frustration leaks into meetings. A leader who is
usually strong becomes unpredictable under pressure. Or conversely, someone shuts down
completely when things get difficult, becoming unavailable precisely when they're needed most.
Why it happens:
Under stress, people revert to their most deeply ingrained patterns. Without self-awareness and
deliberate practice, leaders under pressure often become a less effective version of themselves, at
exactly the moment the team needs them most.
What to do:
Resilience and regulation are learnable skills. A brain-based coaching programme gives leaders
practical tools to interrupt unhelpful patterns, manage their state under pressure, and respond
rather than react. This is one of the highest-return investments available in leadership
development.

Sign 5: There Is No Clear Plan for Who Leads Next
What it looks like:
Your organisation depends heavily on one or two key individuals. You haven't had a serious
conversation about succession. Promising people aren't being developed toward leadership roles,
and there is no structured pipeline of future leaders being built.
Why it happens:
Succession planning gets deprioritised by the urgency of running the business. It feels like a future
problem. But by the time it becomes urgent, it's already too late to do it well.
What to do:
Start with a conversation about your top three to five high-potential individuals. What would it take
for each of them to step into a leadership role? What are the gaps? A people development strategy
doesn't have to be complex, but it does have to be intentional.
